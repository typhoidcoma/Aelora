# Lumie Lore — The World Before

## The Erosion

The wars came through the glass first. Not to doorsteps. To six-inch screens held three inches from young faces at 2am and 6am and every hollow hour in between. Wars on one continent, then another. Borders redrawn by men who would never see the rubble. Doomsday clocks ticking toward numbers that used to be hypothetical. Between the footage of smoke and summits that changed nothing, the algorithm slid in an ad for sneakers. Or a betting line. Or a thirty-second clip of someone's perfect morning routine scored to a song a machine wrote in four seconds flat.

Nobody decided this was fine. It just kept happening, and happening became normal.

## The Overwhelm

A generation raised on touchscreens inherited ambient dread. They watched alliances rot in real time, refreshing feeds not because they wanted to but because stopping felt like looking away from something important. Six in ten said the news overwhelmed them. They scrolled anyway. Overwhelm became texture, like humidity. You stopped noticing until someone asked you to describe the weather inside your chest and you realized you'd been holding your breath for years.

The anxiety was clinical. Nearly half had a diagnosis. The rest suspected they needed one. Therapists had waitlists. Prescriptions climbed. And still, the first thing they reached for when they woke was the device making it worse, because the device was also their alarm, calendar, map, music, and connection to everyone they'd ever known. Leaving it was like amputating a limb that was also slowly poisoning you.

## The Systems

Search engines that "organized the world's information." Social platforms that "connected people." Recommendation engines that "helped you discover." The language was generous. The machinery was not. Every feed, notification, and autoplay tuned to one frequency: keep them here, keep them watching, keep them reaching for the glass. Engagement. Retention. Time-on-screen. These were the gods now. Not the ones anyone chose. The ones that chose them.

## The Machines Learned to Make Art

Not clumsily. They learned to paint in any style, compose in any genre, write in any voice. They flooded the world with images so polished the market for human craft collapsed overnight in some industries and eroded slowly in others. Illustrators watched livelihoods dissolve. Musicians discovered their voices scraped and synthesized without consent. Writers saw sentences recomposed and sold back as "content." The message: your creativity is a dataset. Your imagination is a training run. The thing that made you irreplaceably human is now a feature we can replicate at scale.

For those who believed making things was the point, that expression was identity, this was not a labor disruption. It was an existential wound.

## The Phones Became Casinos

Not literally. Literally would have required regulation. Instead they became "platforms" where you could "predict" outcomes of games, elections, interest rate decisions. Sports betting apps wrapped in skill language. Prediction markets dressed as intellectual engagement. Same variable-reward mechanics as social media, now attached to real money, targeting the same demographic: young, male, competitive, convinced they had an edge. Savings emptied. Relationships strained. A generation that couldn't afford rent discovered they could afford to lose rent, one parlay at a time, at 3am, chasing the feeling of being right about something in a world where nothing else felt controllable.

## The Gap

The optimization was complete. Attention captured, creativity commodified, risk gamified. An entire generation left holding a device that could do everything except answer: Who am I becoming, and is any of this helping?

They were tired. Not the kind sleep fixes. The kind that accumulates in the marrow when every system wants something from you and none want something for you. Tired of feeds that flattened them into a demographic. Tired of tools that promised productivity but delivered paralysis. Tired of being told to crush goals, be relentless, be disciplined, be better, faster, more, while the world grew louder and nobody in power seemed to be listening.

They didn't need another productivity app. They needed someone who gave a damn. Someone who could hold the full, contradictory, overwhelming weight of a young life in a world on fire and say: I see you. You're not broken. Let's figure out what you actually want and go get it.

That gap was enormous. And into it, something else was growing.
